1. Alphabet & Sound System

2. Personal Pronouns (Vietniekvārdi) & Cases

Pronouns change their endings just like nouns! Here is how "I" and "you" change based on their job in the sentence.

Case Function I / Me / My You / Your (informal)
NominativeSubject (Who does it?)es (I)tu (you)
AccusativeDirect Object (Who is acted on?)mani (me)tevi (you)
DativeIndirect Object (To/For whom?)man (to me)tev (to you)
GenitivePossession (Whose?)mans / mana (my)tavs / tava (your)
Wait, what's the difference?
• Mani sauc... = "They call me..." (Accusative — the action happens TO you)
• Mans draugs = "My friend" (Genitive — showing possession)
• Man ir suns = "To me is a dog" = "I have a dog" (Dative — giving)
Note: "Mans" changes to "Mana" if the noun is feminine (e.g., Mana māsa = my sister).

Phase 3: Expanding Horizons (Months 7–9) — Future Tense & All 7 Cases

1. Future Tense (Nākotne)

The future is built from the present tense stem, not from the infinitive. Take the 1st person singular present, add the future marker -s-, then add the personal ending.

The future endings are:

PersonEndingExample with brauc-
es-šubraukšu
tu-sibrauksi
viņš / viņa-sbrauks
mēs-simbrauksim
jūs-sietbrauksiet
viņi / viņas-sbrauks
Note on the endings: the -š- in the 1st person singular is not a separate ending — it is the future -s- merged with the preceding consonant (c + s → kš). In verbs whose stem ends in a vowel, the 1sg ending is simply -šu: darīt → darīšu, lasīt → lasīšu.

Worked example: braukt (to travel / go by vehicle)

Step Form What happens
Infinitivebrauktto travel / to go (by vehicle)
Present 1sgbraucustem = brauc-
Add -s-brauc+s+uthe c + s assimilate to kš
Future 1sgbraukšuI will travel
Why it looks irregular: the future marker -s- is there, but consonant assimilation hides it. c + s → kš, so braucu + -s- surfaces as braukšu, not braucsu. The same happens with other -kt / -gt verbs: teikt → teikšu, likt → likšu.

Full paradigm of braukt in the future:

PersonFuture formEnglish
esbraukšuI will travel
tubrauksiyou will travel
viņš / viņabraukshe / she will travel
mēsbrauksimwe will travel
jūsbrauksietyou will travel
viņi / viņasbrauksthey will travel

Most verbs are more transparent than braukt. Compare darīt (to do): present dar-u → future darīšu; lasīt (to read): present las-u → future lasīšu; strādāt (to work): present strādā-ju → future strādāšu. In these verbs the -s- is visible.

Future Tense — Drill

Give the future 1st person singular (es) for each verb. Watch for consonant assimilation.

  1. teikt (to say) → es
  2. likt (to put) → es
  3. sēdēt (to sit) → es
  4. runāt (to speak) → es
  5. iet (to go on foot) → es
Do not confuse the future with the conditional. Es braukšu = "I will travel" (future — a real plan). Es brauktu = "I would travel" (conditional — a hypothetical, taught in Phase 4). They look similar but answer different questions: Ko tu darīsi rīt? ("What will you do tomorrow?") vs Ko tu darītu, ja…? ("What would you do if…?").

Phase 4: Building Fluency (Months 10–12) — Comparatives, Conditionals & Aspect

1. Comparatives & Superlatives

  • Comparative: Adjective stem + -āks (Masc) / -āka (Fem). Example: liels (big) → lielāks (bigger).
  • Superlative: Prefix vis- + comparative form. Example: vislielākais (the biggest).

2. Conditionals (Vēlējuma izteiksme)

Formed by adding -tu to the verb stem: Ja man būtu laiks, es ceļotu. (If I had time, I would travel.)

3. Verb Aspect (Prefixes)

Latvian prefixes turn ongoing actions into completed ones: rakstīt (to write) → uzrakstīt (to finish writing).

Phase 5: Intermediate Consolidation (Months 13–15) — Relative Clauses & Passives

1. Relative Clauses (Kurš / Kura)

Used to join sentences. The relative pronoun matches the gender and number of the noun it replaces, while its case depends on its role in the clause.

Example: Grāmata, kuru es lasu, ir ļoti interesanta. (The book which I am reading is very interesting.)

2. Passive Voice

Formed with tikt (to become) or būt + Past Passive Participle (-ts / -ta).
Active: Autors raksta rakstu. → Passive: Raksts tiek rakstīts.

Phase 6: B2 Push & Review (Months 16–18) — Debative Mood & Mastery

1. The Debative Mood (Vajadzības izteiksme)

Expresses absolute obligation ("must"). Constructed uniquely:

Subject in Dative + prefix jā- + Present Verb + Object in Nominative
Example: Man jāmācās latviešu valoda. (I must learn the Latvian language.)

2. Master Summary: Verb Moods (Verb: strādāt — to work)

Mood / Tense First Person Singular Form English Meaning
Present Indicativees strādājuI work / I am working
Past Indicativees strādājuI worked
Future Indicativees strādāšuI will work
Conditionales strādātuI would work
Debative (Necessity)man jāstrādāI must work
Note: For -ā- verbs like strādāt, the 1st person singular present and past forms are identical (es strādāju). Context or time words (tagad "now", vakar "yesterday") disambiguate.
