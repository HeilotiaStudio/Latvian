Phase 1: Foundations (Months 1–3) — Alphabet, Basic Verbs & Cases

1. Alphabet & Sound System

Latvian uses a modified Latin alphabet with 33 letters. Vowel length is phonemic (long vowels like ā, ē, ī, ū last twice as long and change word meanings). Stress almost always falls on the first syllable.

Welcome & Introductions (Sveicieni un iepazīšanās)

1. Greetings (Sveicieni)

Latvian English When to use it
Labdien!Good day!Neutral, safe, works any time of day. The default polite greeting.
Labrīt!Good morning!Until roughly 11:00.
Labvakar!Good evening!From roughly 18:00 onward.
Sveiki!Hi! / Hello!Informal — friends, peers, people your own age.
Sveiki visiem!Hello everyone!Greeting a group.
Čau!Hi! / Bye!Very informal — used both for hello and goodbye.

Note: Latvian has no single "good afternoon." Labdien covers the whole daytime period and is the safest choice if you are unsure.

2. Noun Genders — Singular & Plural (Nominative)

Gender is visible in the nominative singular ending. The plural ending depends on the gender and on which singular ending the noun has. Learn the singular + plural together as a pair.

Masculine Nouns

Singular ending Singular example Plural ending Plural example English
-sgalds-igalditable → tables
-šceļš-iceļiroad → roads
-iskaķis-ikaķicat → cats
Rule of thumb: all masculine nouns take -i in the plural. The singular -s / -š / -is is dropped and replaced by -i. (galds → galdi, ceļš → ceļi, kaķis → kaķi.)

Feminine Nouns

Singular ending Singular example Plural ending Plural example English
-amāja-asmājashouse → houses
-emeitene-esmeitenesgirl → girls
-szivs-iszivisfish → fish(es)
Rule of thumb: feminine nouns in -a take -as; in -e take -es. The small group in -s (like zivs) takes -is — and note the singular and plural are spelled the same (zivs / zivis), differing only in vowel length and pronunciation.
Watch the -s trap: a noun ending in -s is not automatically masculine. Galds is masculine (plural galdi), but zivs is feminine (plural zivis). You have to learn the gender with the noun — which is why the tables above pair singular and plural from the start.

4. Present Tense of būt (to be) & Accusative Case

Now that you know the pronouns, here is how they combine with būt (to be) — and, alongside it, the accusative endings you will need for direct objects.

Person Pronoun & būt Plural būt Accusative Singular Endings (Direct Object)
1st Persones esmu (I am)mēs esam (we are)Masc. -s → -u (galds → galdu)
2nd Persontu esi (you are)jūs esat (you are)Masc. -is → -i (kaķis → kaķi)
3rd Personviņš/viņa ir (he/she is)viņi/viņas ir (they are)Fem. -a → -u (māja → māju) / -e → -i
Gender note: Many Latvian nouns for people have a masculine and a feminine form. A male speaker says students; a female speaker says studente. The same pattern applies to skolotājs / skolotāja (teacher), darbinieks / darbiniece (employee), and many others.

Phase 2: Everyday Survival (Months 4–6) — Locative, Genitive, Dative & Past Tense

1. The Locative Case (Lokatīvs — Location)

Answers kur? ("where?"). Replaces the noun's ending with a long vowel.

Nominative Locative Form English Equivalent Nominative Locative Form English Equivalent
galds (-s)galdāon the tableRīga (-a)Rīgāin Riga
skapis (-is)skapīin the closetvirtuve (-e)virtuvēin the kitchen

2. Genitive & Dative Cases

  • Genitive (Ģenitīvs): Answers kā? ("whose?"). Masculine nouns take -a, feminine take -as / -es. Also used with prepositions like bez (without) and no (from).
  • Dative (Datīvs): Answers kam? ("to whom?"). Expresses age and possession: Man ir auto (I have a car) or Man ir 20 gadi (I am 20 years old).

Pronoun Datives: man, tev, viņam, viņai, mums, jums, viņiem, viņām.

3. The Simple Past Tense (Pagātne)

Formed by dropping present endings and adding past suffixes: -ju/-u (es), -ji (tu), -ja (viņš/viņa), -jām/-ām (mēs), -jāt/-āt (jūs).

Phase 4: Building Fluency (Months 10–12) — Comparatives, Conditionals & Aspect

1. Comparatives & Superlatives

  • Comparative: Adjective stem + -āks (Masc) / -āka (Fem). Example: liels (big) → lielāks (bigger).
  • Superlative: Prefix vis- + comparative form. Example: vislielākais (the biggest).

2. Conditionals (Vēlējuma izteiksme)

Formed by adding -tu to the verb stem: Ja man būtu laiks, es ceļotu. (If I had time, I would travel.)

3. Verb Aspect (Prefixes)

Latvian prefixes turn ongoing actions into completed ones: rakstīt (to write) → uzrakstīt (to finish writing).

Phase 5: Intermediate Consolidation (Months 13–15) — Relative Clauses & Passives

1. Relative Clauses (Kurš / Kura)

Used to join sentences. The relative pronoun matches the gender and number of the noun it replaces, while its case depends on its role in the clause.

Example: Grāmata, kuru es lasu, ir ļoti interesanta. (The book which I am reading is very interesting.)

2. Passive Voice

Formed with tikt (to become) or būt + Past Passive Participle (-ts / -ta).
Active: Autors raksta rakstu. → Passive: Raksts tiek rakstīts.

Phase 6: B2 Push & Review (Months 16–18) — Debative Mood & Mastery

1. The Debative Mood (Vajadzības izteiksme)

Expresses absolute obligation ("must"). Constructed uniquely:

Subject in Dative + prefix jā- + Present Verb + Object in Nominative
Example: Man jāmācās latviešu valoda. (I must learn the Latvian language.)

2. Master Summary: Verb Moods (Verb: strādāt — to work)

Mood / Tense First Person Singular Form English Meaning
Present Indicativees strādājuI work / I am working
Past Indicativees strādājuI worked
Future Indicativees strādāšuI will work
Conditionales strādātuI would work
Debative (Necessity)man jāstrādāI must work
Note: For -ā- verbs like strādāt, the 1st person singular present and past forms are identical (es strādāju). Context or time words (tagad "now", vakar "yesterday") disambiguate.
