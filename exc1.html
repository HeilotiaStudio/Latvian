Latvian Pronouns — Interactive Practice

I · you · we · he · she · they — and the object forms (me, him, her, them)
How to use: type your answer in each blank, then press Check answers under the exercise. Accents and case are ignored (you can type abol for ābols), but the actual word must be right — mani and man are different answers.
Why pronouns look "different" in different sentences: English uses the same word me for both "he sees me" and "he gives me a book." Latvian does not. It uses a direct-object form (accusative) for the first, and an indirect-object form (dative) for the second. This sheet teaches both, separately.

2. Direct-Object Pronouns (accusative — "whom do I see / like / have?")

Used after verbs like redzēt (to see), mīlēt (to love), gaidīt (to wait for), pazīt (to know a person).

EnglishLatvianExampleTranslation
memaniViņš redz mani.He sees me.
you (sg.)teviEs gaidu tevi.I am waiting for you.
you (formal / pl.)jūsEs redzu jūs.I see you.
himviņuEs pazīstu viņu.I know him.
herviņuEs mīlu viņu.I love her.
usmūsViņi gaida mūs.They are waiting for us.
them (masc./mixed)viņusEs redzu viņus.I see them.
them (all female)viņasEs redzu viņas.I see them (all female).
Watch out: viņu means both "him" and "her" — context decides. And viņas is both the subject "they (all female)" and the object "them (all female)." Word order and verb agreement tell you which.

3. Indirect-Object Pronouns (dative — "to whom / for whom?")

Used after verbs like dot (to give), teikt (to say to), palīdzēt (to help), zvanīt (to call), and in the pattern Man ir… (I have…).

EnglishLatvianExampleTranslation
to memanViņš man palīdz.He helps me.
to you (sg.)tevEs tev zvanu.I am calling you.
to you (formal / pl.)jumsEs jums palīdzēšu.I will help you.
to himviņamEs viņam teicu.I told him.
to herviņaiEs viņai palīdzu.I help her.
to usmumsViņš mums palīdz.He helps us.
to them (masc./mixed)viņiemEs viņiem saku.I say to them.
to them (all female)viņāmEs viņām saku.I say to them (all female).
Key pattern: Man ir = "I have" (literally "to me is"). So Tev ir = you have, Viņam ir = he has, Mums ir = we have. This is the single most useful dative pattern for beginners — and it's why "I have" is not Es ir.
